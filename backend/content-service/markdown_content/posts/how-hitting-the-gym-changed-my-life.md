Hey long time no see! How are you today?

The weather in Hanoi has been pouring all week, especially today. The streets are flooded everywhere, making commuting a real hassle. Luckily, I’ve got the day off—no school today, just deep work, hitting the gym, and eating clean.

Do you play any sports? For me, sports have always been a huge part of my life.

## From Badminton to Basketball: Finding My Athletic Roots

Back in secondary school, I used to be a chubby kid who played badminton—and surprisingly, I got really good at it. I owe a ton to my mom because she always supported me and bought me my gear, which I’m still truly grateful for. In my final year of secondary school, I won a gold medal in a school tournament and even had the opportunity to compete in the national youth cup.

But in high school, I stopped playing. There were several reasons, but the biggest was that my teammates started quitting one by one. Without a crew, I didn't win any titles in high school, and that’s when I pivoted to basketball.

It didn't take me long to pick it up. Thanks to decent athleticism and body control, I adapted pretty fast. It’s the sport I still play to this day. I’ve joined quite a few tournaments, though my highest finish is still just runner-up—still missing that championship ring, lmao.

## Stepping Into the Iron Game: Day One

Besides basketball, I started hitting the gym right after finishing high school. Back then, my body wasn't too skinny, but it wasn't big either—for some reason, my lower body had way more mass compared to my upper body.

## The Gym Doesn't Lie

The gym doesn't lie. Unlike other sports where you can pick up the fundamentals in a month and see clear progress soon after, lifting demands relentless effort. Even after three months, you might only see a subtle shift. What you put into your body and how hard you train reflect right back in the mirror.

### The "One-More" Mindset

The "one-more" mindset. If I want to get shredded, I have to push to failure. In every set, taking my body to absolute failure gives my muscles a reason to adapt and grow. I know going to failure every time might not be the most scientifically optimal way to program, but it's not just about muscle gain—it's about mental fortitude and proving that pushing through discomfort makes you stronger.

On days when I don't feel like showing up and just want to sleep in, I still drag myself out of bed. The heaviest weight you will ever lift is yourself.

## Beyond the Physical Grind: 1.5 Years Later

Beyond the physical grind, the gym has taught me so much more: how to eat with intention, how to truly control my body, and how to treat myself and those around me with more patience and respect.

I’ve started applying progressive overload to everything outside the gym—stacking small, incremental improvements into my work, learning, and character. Over time, I realized that lifting is like a parallel version of life itself: quiet discipline, consistent effort, and delayed gratification. And one day, I genuinely want to step onto a natural bodybuilding stage to test my ultimate limits.

![Physique progress after 1.5 years of consistent lifting](./images/gym-one-and-half-year-progress.webp)

I know this might sound a little cocky, but I love the way people look at me; I want people to be attracted to my physique. Still, it’s not just about turning heads—it’s about impressing myself. Striving to get better every single day, building real discipline, pushing harder, and achieving more.

If you don’t hit the gym right now, I just wanna tell: **“Don’t be a skinny bitch.”**
