> **TL;DR:** Summer’s winding down. Packing my things for Hanoi after two months home, looking at the worn-out backpack Mom carefully mended and the boxes of food she neatly stacked for me.

Hi there, how was your summer?

If this is your first time visiting my blog, welcome! You can learn a bit more [about me](#about).

Looking back, this summer was probably one of the most productive breaks I’ve ever had, but easily the bittersweetest.

In July, right after wrapping up my sophomore year, I landed an internship at a small tech company. The stipend was small, but my hopes were high—I walked out of the interview feeling confident. The first two weeks were packed with intensive onboarding. My mentor was great, my teammates were sharp, and together we even built an internal Werewolf game platform. I soaked up everything from testing and CI/CD pipelines to API design.

Then came the end-of-training assessment.

It hit hard. I didn’t pass. And just like that, after barely two weeks, my internship ended before it truly began—no continuation, no stipend. It stung, but I refused to let myself spiral. I channeled that frustration into registering for a massive AI hackathon (a story for another post), and then packed my bags to head home.

August brought a quieter rhythm. Back home with my parents, my days revolved around LeetCode, learning new programming concepts, and going on long runs. Mid-month, I took a quick two-day trip to Hanoi for another hackathon—my second one ever. The energy was electric, the atmosphere was incredible, and we ended up finishing in the top 20.

I returned home just in time for my grandfather’s death anniversary and the long National Day holiday. During those five days off, I entered a big 3x3 basketball tournament and brought home a runner-up medal.

Yet, good days always fly by too quickly. With only four days left of vacation, an ache of dread began to settle in. I didn’t want to leave, but I had to constantly remind myself to just stay present and cherish every moment.

During those final days, my mom took my old, beaten-up backpack—one I had been planning to replace—washed it, and quietly stitched it back together. She’s remarkably skilled with needle and thread. Holding it in my hands, neatly mended, with her telling me, "Whenever it wears out, just bring it home and I'll fix it again," I realized I didn't want a new bag anymore.

Then came the food prep: containers of caramelized pork, rich beef stew, snacks, and several packs of my favorite coffee that she bought just so I’d have enough to drink when studying.

Two months at home, packed into one afternoon. The best food in the world, stuffed into a patched bag with an affection that only made walking out the door ten times harder. But time arrived, and I had to pack everything up and return to Hanoi for the new semester.

People ask why I call this summer bittersweet despite everything I did. It’s because deep down, I know this was likely the last summer I could truly pause and rest. Next year, the grind for another internship starts all over again, followed shortly by graduation. Summers like this one will never come around again.

I don’t know yet if I’ll achieve all the big goals I’m chasing, but right now, all I really want is to make my mom happy and proud.

Sitting in Hanoi tonight, unpacking her cooking, I just miss her so much.
